Blue Team

Detect. Defend. Respond. Protect.

Blue Teamers are the guardians of the digital realm — cybersecurity professionals who monitor, detect, investigate, and respond to threats 24/7. They build and operate the controls, tools, and processes that keep organizations safe, turning attacker activity into actionable intelligence.

01
Security Operations Center (SOC Analyst)
Beginner

SOC Analysts are the first line of defense, monitoring security alerts 24/7, triaging incidents, and investigating suspicious activity. Tier 1 handles initial alert triage; Tier 2 conducts deep investigation; Tier 3 leads threat hunting and complex incident response.

SplunkQRadarMicrosoft SentinelAlert TriageSIEM
What You Do Daily
  • Monitor SIEM dashboards around the clock and investigate all triggered security alerts
  • Triage alerts quickly — distinguish true positives from false positives to reduce analyst fatigue
  • Investigate suspicious logins, malware detections, network anomalies, and data access events
  • Escalate confirmed incidents to Tier 2/IR teams with documented timeline and initial findings
02
Threat Detection & Monitoring
Intermediate

Threat Detection specialists build and maintain the detection logic powering the SOC. They write detection rules, configure alerts, tune SIEM platforms, and analyze telemetry across endpoints, networks, and cloud to surface attacker behaviors before damage is done.

Sigma RulesYARAElastic StackKQLChronicle
What You Do Daily
  • Write Sigma rules and SIEM queries to detect specific ATT&CK techniques in raw log data
  • Map current detection coverage against MITRE ATT&CK framework to identify blind spots
  • Tune alert thresholds and exceptions to reduce false positive noise without losing coverage
  • Analyze multi-source logs (firewall, AD, EDR, cloud) for correlated attack indicators
03
Incident Response
Intermediate

Incident Responders are called in when breaches happen. They contain active attacks, eradicate malware, investigate the full compromise scope, and restore systems — all under intense time pressure while following structured IR playbooks from containment through full recovery.

DFIRVolatilityFTKNIST IR FrameworkTheHive
What You Do Daily
  • Execute containment immediately — isolate infected hosts, block IPs, revoke compromised credentials
  • Perform memory and disk forensics to reconstruct attacker actions across the timeline
  • Identify patient zero and map the full lateral movement and persistence chain
  • Lead post-incident reviews and deliver comprehensive IR reports to technical and executive audiences
04
Digital Forensics
Intermediate

Digital Forensics analysts investigate evidence left by attackers, insider threats, or security incidents. They recover deleted files, analyze disk images, examine browser artifacts, registry hives, and volatile memory to reconstruct exactly what happened, when, and how.

AutopsyFTK ImagerVolatilitySleuth KitKAPE
What You Do Daily
  • Create forensically sound disk images maintaining strict chain-of-custody documentation
  • Analyze Windows artifacts — prefetch, Amcache, registry, event logs, LNK files, NTFS journals
  • Recover deleted files and carve data from unallocated disk space with file carving tools
  • Analyze memory dumps for running processes, network connections, injected code, and credentials
05
Threat Hunting
Advanced

Threat Hunters proactively search environments for hidden attackers that have evaded automated detection. Instead of waiting for alerts, they form hypotheses based on threat intelligence and ATT&CK TTPs, then manually investigate telemetry to uncover threats lurking in the environment.

MITRE ATT&CKVelociraptorKQLThreat IntelOSQuery
What You Do Daily
  • Form and document threat hypotheses based on current threat intelligence and ATT&CK techniques
  • Hunt for suspicious parent-child process relationships and LOLBin (living-off-the-land) abuse
  • Search EDR telemetry for beacon-like C2 traffic patterns in DNS, HTTP, and HTTPS logs
  • Convert confirmed hunt findings into new SIEM detection rules to improve automated coverage
06
Malware Analysis
Advanced

Malware Analysts dissect malicious software — ransomware, RATs, stealers, rootkits — to understand how they work, what they do, and how to detect and remove them. They combine static code inspection with dynamic sandbox analysis to extract IOCs and build detection signatures.

IDA ProGhidrax64dbgCuckoo SandboxANY.RUN
What You Do Daily
  • Perform static analysis: hash identification, YARA scanning, PE header inspection, string extraction
  • Execute malware in isolated sandboxes and document all behavioral indicators (file, registry, network)
  • Reverse-engineer obfuscated or packed malware using IDA Pro or Ghidra to understand core logic
  • Extract and share IOCs (IPs, domains, hashes, mutex names) for detection and threat intel enrichment
07
Security Information & Event Management (SIEM)
Intermediate

SIEM Engineers architect, deploy, and maintain the central logging and alerting platform that powers the entire SOC. They onboard log sources, write correlation rules, build dashboards, manage data retention policies, and ensure comprehensive visibility across the entire environment.

SplunkMicrosoft SentinelElastic SIEMQRadarLogRhythm
What You Do Daily
  • Onboard new log sources — firewalls, AD, EDR, cloud, endpoints — with proper field normalization
  • Write multi-source correlation rules to detect multi-stage attack patterns across log pipelines
  • Build SOC dashboards for real-time visibility into threats, alert volumes, and environment health
  • Tune rules continuously to maintain signal quality and manage SIEM storage and performance
08
Endpoint Security (EDR/XDR)
Intermediate

Endpoint Security specialists manage and operate EDR/XDR platforms deployed across all workstations and servers. They investigate endpoint alerts, analyze malicious process behavior, configure detection policies, and use rich telemetry for threat hunting and forensic investigations.

CrowdStrike FalconSentinelOneMicrosoft DefenderCarbon BlackCortex XDR
What You Do Daily
  • Investigate EDR process tree alerts — command-line arguments, file drops, network connections
  • Deploy and tune detection policies across thousands of endpoints while minimizing false positives
  • Use EDR telemetry to hunt for fileless malware, memory injection, and living-off-the-land attacks
  • Remotely isolate and remediate infected endpoints during active incidents to contain spread
09
Network Security Monitoring
Intermediate

Network Security Monitoring analysts watch all traffic for intrusion, exfiltration, lateral movement, and C2 communication. They use tools like Zeek, Suricata, and full packet capture to analyze flows and detect threats that endpoint tools miss.

ZeekSuricataWiresharkSecurity OnionPCAP Analysis
What You Do Daily
  • Analyze NetFlow and full packet captures for anomalous traffic patterns and protocol abuse
  • Write custom Suricata/Snort IDS rules to detect known attack signatures and novel behaviors
  • Investigate beaconing patterns in DNS and HTTPS traffic indicating active C2 communication
  • Monitor for large/unusual data transfers that may signal active exfiltration campaigns
10
Vulnerability Management
Beginner

Vulnerability Management analysts continuously scan infrastructure for known security flaws, prioritize them based on risk and exploitability, and track remediation to closure. They bridge security findings and IT teams who must implement patches and configuration fixes.

NessusQualysRapid7 InsightVMCVSS ScoringTenable.io
What You Do Daily
  • Schedule and execute authenticated vulnerability scans across all asset classes regularly
  • Prioritize vulnerabilities by CVSS score, exploit availability, asset criticality, and exposure
  • Track remediation SLAs by asset owner and escalate overdue items to management
  • Validate successful remediation by rescanning fixed assets and updating the risk register
11
Patch Management
Beginner

Patch Management specialists ensure software, OS, and firmware updates are systematically deployed across the entire organization. They maintain patching schedules, test patches in staging, emergency-patch critical CVEs, and report compliance metrics to security leadership.

WSUSSCCM / IntuneAnsibleJamfPatch Tuesday
What You Do Daily
  • Maintain a complete, current software and firmware inventory across all managed assets
  • Test patches in staging environments before production deployment to avoid service disruptions
  • Coordinate emergency patching for critical CVEs like Log4Shell and PrintNightmare within SLA
  • Track and report patch compliance rates and highlight unpatched critical assets to management
12
Identity & Access Management (IAM)
Intermediate

IAM specialists manage who has access to what across the enterprise — enforcing least privilege, managing the full user lifecycle, administering MFA and SSO, and operating Privileged Access Management (PAM) tools to secure high-value administrator accounts.

Active DirectoryOktaCyberArk PAMAzure AD / EntraSailPoint
What You Do Daily
  • Manage user lifecycle — provisioning, deprovisioning, access reviews for joiners/movers/leavers
  • Enforce MFA and conditional access policies across all critical systems and cloud platforms
  • Monitor and secure privileged accounts with PAM tools, session recording, and just-in-time access
  • Detect and investigate anomalous account activity — off-hours logins, privilege escalation, lateral moves
13
Security Compliance Monitoring
Beginner

Security Compliance Monitors ensure the organization continuously meets regulatory requirements and internal security policies. They audit controls, track compliance metrics, maintain audit evidence, and identify policy violations before formal external assessments find them first.

ISO 27001PCI DSSNIST CSFHIPAAAudit Evidence
What You Do Daily
  • Monitor systems and configurations continuously for deviations from approved security baselines
  • Collect, organize, and maintain compliance evidence (logs, screenshots) throughout the year
  • Track remediation of audit findings with control owners and verify closure before re-assessment
  • Prepare compliance dashboards and status reports for CISO and audit committee presentations
14
Email Security & Phishing Detection
Beginner

Email Security specialists protect the most-targeted attack vector in any organization. They manage email gateways, analyze reported phishing emails, build detection rules for new campaigns, implement anti-spoofing controls, and measure employee awareness through simulation campaigns.

ProofpointMimecastMicrosoft DefenderDMARC/SPF/DKIMPhishMe
What You Do Daily
  • Analyze suspicious emails reported by employees — headers, links, attachments, sender reputation
  • Configure and fine-tune email gateway rules to block phishing, BEC, and spam at scale
  • Implement and monitor DMARC, DKIM, and SPF records to prevent domain spoofing and impersonation
  • Design and run monthly phishing simulation campaigns to measure and improve employee click rates
