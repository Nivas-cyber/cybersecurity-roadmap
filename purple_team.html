Purple Team

Collaborate. Simulate. Detect. Improve.

Purple Team practitioners bridge the gap between attackers and defenders. They facilitate collaboration between Red and Blue teams — using controlled attack simulations to validate detection coverage, expose blind spots, and drive continuous, measurable improvements to the security posture.

01
Red Team & Blue Team Collaboration
Advanced

Purple Team facilitators structure joint exercises where Red and Blue work together openly — sharing attack techniques and detection results in real time. Unlike traditional red team engagements, there are no secrets: both sides learn collaboratively to accelerate security improvement cycles.

Joint ExercisesTabletop (TTX)Knowledge TransferMITRE ATT&CKVECTR
What You Do Daily
  • Coordinate tabletop exercises where Red and Blue teams work through attack scenarios together
  • Run live "show and tell" sessions — Red executes a technique while Blue attempts to detect it
  • Document collaborative findings: what was detected, what was missed, root cause, and remediation
  • Build shared vocabulary and mutual understanding between offensive and defensive practitioners
02
Attack Simulation & Validation
Intermediate

Attack Simulation specialists execute controlled, precise attack techniques to validate whether security controls — SIEM rules, EDR policies, network sensors — actually detect simulated behavior. This provides hard, empirical evidence of what works and what has silent gaps.

Atomic Red TeamCALDERAVECTRBreach & Attack SimSafeBreach
What You Do Daily
  • Run Atomic Red Team test cases to simulate specific MITRE ATT&CK techniques against live systems
  • Validate that SIEM and EDR alerts fired correctly and with sufficient fidelity for the executed technique
  • Track simulation results in VECTR to build a longitudinal coverage map over time
  • Identify silent detection gaps where attacks complete successfully with no alert generated
03
Detection Engineering
Advanced

Detection Engineers design, build, and maintain the detection logic that powers the SOC. They translate attacker TTPs into working SIEM rules and behavioral analytics — combining offensive knowledge of how attacks work with defensive understanding of how to catch them reliably in noisy log data.

Sigma RulesKQL / SPLYARA-LDetection-as-CodeGit
What You Do Daily
  • Translate red team findings into new SIEM detection rules the same day as discovery
  • Write platform-agnostic Sigma rules and automatically convert to Splunk SPL, Microsoft KQL, etc.
  • Test every detection against real attack data to verify accuracy and minimize false positives
  • Maintain a detection-as-code pipeline with version control, testing, and automated deployment
04
Threat Emulation (MITRE ATT&CK Based)
Advanced

Threat Emulation specialists simulate the exact TTPs of real-world threat actors — APT groups, ransomware gangs, nation-state actors — mapped precisely to MITRE ATT&CK. They test whether the organization can detect the adversaries most likely to target their specific industry.

MITRE ATT&CKCTI ReportsCALDERACobalt StrikeATT&CK Navigator
What You Do Daily
  • Research threat intelligence on adversaries known to target your specific industry vertical
  • Build TTP-accurate emulation plans based on real malware samples and documented APT behavior
  • Execute emulation scenarios in controlled windows and measure detection/prevention/response coverage
  • Map all emulation results to ATT&CK Navigator heat maps for visual coverage gap presentation
05
Security Control Testing
Intermediate

Security Control Testers systematically verify every security control — firewalls, DLP, AV, MFA, UEBA — is working as designed against real attack techniques, not just configuration reviews. They ensure controls provide actual protection, not just paper compliance.

BAS PlatformsPicus SecuritySafeBreachControl FrameworksRisk Scoring
What You Do Daily
  • Map all security controls to the specific threats and ATT&CK techniques they are designed to block
  • Execute real attack payloads against production controls and document block/alert/miss outcomes
  • Run continuous automated BAS (Breach and Attack Simulation) to monitor control drift over time
  • Report control failures with quantified business risk context to prioritize and drive remediation
06
Adversary Simulation (Real-world Attack Scenarios)
Expert

Adversary Simulation specialists build and run end-to-end attack scenarios mirroring how a real advanced attacker would compromise the organization — from initial phishing to domain compromise — while simultaneously having the Blue Team practice detection and response in real time.

Full Kill ChainThreat-Intel DrivenTIBER-EUCBESTDORA TLPT
What You Do Daily

  • Facilitate thorough debriefs on what was detected, delayed, and missed — with concrete improvement actions
07
Detection Tuning & Optimization
Intermediate

Detection Tuning specialists analyze the quality of existing SIEM rules — identifying rules generating too many false positives (alert fatigue) or too few alerts (missed attacks). They optimize the precision-recall balance to ensure the SOC receives high-quality, high-fidelity, actionable alerts.

False Positive ReductionAlert QualitySIEM OptimizationPrecision/RecallMetrics
What You Do Daily
  • Audit all detection rules for their false positive rate, volume, and actionability scores
  • Add contextual exclusions to noisy rules without creating exploitable blind spots
  • Test proposed rule changes against historical attack data to predict impact before live deployment
  • Track and report SOC alert quality metrics — MTTD, false positive %, detection coverage over time
08
Gap Analysis (Identify Security Weaknesses)
Intermediate

Gap Analysis specialists map the organization's actual detection and prevention capabilities against the full threat landscape — primarily the MITRE ATT&CK framework. They produce heat maps showing which techniques are covered, partially covered, and completely blind spots that need urgent attention.

ATT&CK NavigatorCoverage MappingVECTRRisk PrioritizationGap Reports
What You Do Daily
  • Map all existing SIEM detections and EDR policies to their corresponding ATT&CK technique IDs
  • Run simulation tests for uncovered techniques to confirm gaps are real and not just unmapped
  • Prioritize gap remediation based on threat actor relevance, exploitation frequency, and business impact
  • Present coverage heat maps and prioritized gap reports to security leadership for budget decisions
09

Continuous Security Improvement specialists own the long-term program that tracks, measures, and drives security maturity over time. They build improvement roadmaps, track KPIs and KRIs, prioritize remediation by risk, and ensure every purple team exercise produces documented, measurable improvements.

Security MetricsCMMIPDCA CycleRisk KPIsMaturity Models

SOAR specialists automate repetitive SOC workflows and build response playbooks that execute automatically when specific threats are detected. They dramatically reduce analyst workload, accelerate mean time to respond, and ensure consistent, documented handling of all common incident types.

Key Tool
Learn Atomic Red Team and VECTR for running and tracking attack simulations at scale.
Top Cert
CPTIA (Certified Purple Team Intelligence Analyst) and PNPT are well-regarded in this space.
