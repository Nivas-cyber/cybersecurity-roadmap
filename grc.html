GOVERNANCE & COMPLIANCE

GRC

Govern. Assess Risk. Ensure Compliance.

GRC professionals are the architects of security policy, risk management, and regulatory compliance. They ensure the organization has the right frameworks, processes, and controls in place — managing risk at a business level and building a culture of governance from the top down.

01
Security Governance (Policies & Frameworks)
Beginner

Security Governance specialists establish the rules of the road for cybersecurity across the entire organization. They develop security policies, standards, and procedures — and align the security program to industry frameworks like NIST CSF, ISO 27001, and CIS Controls for a structured, measurable approach.

NIST CSFISO 27001CIS ControlsPolicy WritingGRC Platforms
What You Do Daily
  • Develop and maintain the organization's full information security policy suite from scratch
  • Align security controls to chosen frameworks and track coverage using GRC platform tooling
  • Run governance committee meetings to review policy exceptions, new risks, and program updates
  • Ensure all security policies are clearly communicated, understood, and acknowledged by staff
02
Risk Management (Risk Assessment & Treatment)
Intermediate

Risk Management professionals identify, assess, and prioritize cybersecurity risks to the organization's assets and operations. They run formal risk assessments, quantify risk in business terms using FAIR or risk matrices, and work with leadership to decide how each risk should be treated — mitigated, accepted, transferred, or avoided.

Risk RegisterFAIR ModelRisk MatrixISO 31000Risk Scoring
What You Do Daily
  • Conduct formal risk assessments for new systems, major projects, and significant business changes
  • Maintain and continuously update the enterprise information security risk register
  • Quantify risks using the FAIR model to present in financial impact terms to executive leadership
  • Facilitate risk treatment decisions and document risk acceptance with clear ownership accountability
03
Compliance Management (ISO 27001, NIST, GDPR)
Intermediate

Compliance Managers ensure the organization meets all applicable regulatory and contractual security obligations. They manage multi-framework compliance programs covering GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, SOC 2, ISO 27001, and more — coordinating audits, tracking gaps, and maintaining evidence year-round.

GDPRHIPAAPCI DSSSOC 2ISO 27001
What You Do Daily
  • Map all applicable regulatory and contractual requirements to existing security controls
  • Manage the compliance calendar — assessment schedules, renewal deadlines, evidence collection windows
  • Coordinate external audit preparation and compile complete evidence packages for auditors
  • Track and remediate compliance control gaps with technical control owners and IT teams
04
Security Auditing
Intermediate

Security Auditors conduct systematic, independent reviews of security controls, processes, and systems to verify they are designed and operating effectively. They perform internal audits against policies and frameworks, identify weaknesses, and provide objective findings before external auditors arrive.

CISA CertificationAudit MethodologyISACAControl TestingInternal Audit
What You Do Daily
  • Plan and execute internal security audits against policies, frameworks, and regulatory requirements
  • Test control design effectiveness — not just documented, but verify controls are actually working
  • Interview control owners and review evidence samples to assess operational effectiveness
  • Report all findings with risk ratings, root cause analysis, and specific actionable remediation steps
05
Third-Party / Vendor Risk Management
Intermediate

Third-Party Risk Management specialists assess and monitor the cybersecurity posture of vendors, suppliers, and partners. With supply chain attacks rising dramatically, they ensure all third parties with access to data or systems meet required security standards before and after onboarding.

SIG QuestionnairesSecurityScorecardBitSightVendor TiersTPRM
What You Do Daily
  • Assess new vendors through security questionnaires, certifications review, and risk scoring tools
  • Tier all vendors by risk level based on data sensitivity, access level, and business criticality
  • Monitor ongoing vendor security posture continuously with TPRM platform external scanning
  • Manage right-to-audit contractual clauses and escalate critical vendor security findings to leadership
06
Business Continuity Planning (BCP)
Intermediate

BCP specialists ensure the organization can continue critical business operations during major disruptions — cyberattacks, natural disasters, pandemics, or infrastructure failures. They define recovery strategies, document continuity plans, and test them through tabletop and live exercises.

BCP PlansBusiness Impact AnalysisRTO / RPOISO 22301Crisis Management
What You Do Daily
  • Conduct Business Impact Analyses (BIAs) to identify all critical processes and their dependencies
  • Define RTOs and RPOs for every critical system and business function in collaboration with owners
  • Write and maintain practical BCP plans that operational teams can execute under actual disruption
  • Run annual tabletop and live exercises to validate plan effectiveness and close identified gaps
07
Disaster Recovery (DR)
Intermediate

Disaster Recovery specialists focus on restoring IT systems and data after catastrophic failures — ransomware, data center outages, hardware failures. They design backup architectures, define restoration procedures, test recovery processes, and ensure systems can be brought back within defined objectives.

Backup ArchitectureCloud DRFailover TestingNIST SP 800-343-2-1 Rule
What You Do Daily
  • Design comprehensive backup and replication strategies (3-2-1 rule) for all critical systems
  • Implement and regularly test failover procedures to secondary and cloud environments
  • Conduct DR tests — restore from backup in isolated environment and measure actual recovery time
  • Document detailed DR runbooks and ensure incident response and IT teams are trained on them
08
Data Protection & Privacy
Intermediate

Data Protection professionals ensure the organization handles personal and sensitive data lawfully and securely. They implement privacy programs, manage data subject requests, conduct Privacy Impact Assessments, and ensure compliance with GDPR, CCPA, and other global privacy regulations.

GDPRCCPA / CPRAData ClassificationPrivacy by DesignDPO Role
What You Do Daily
  • Maintain records of processing activities (RoPA) for all personal data as required by GDPR Art. 30
  • Conduct Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs/DPIAs) for all new systems handling personal data
  • Handle Data Subject Access Requests (DSARs) accurately and within legal response timeframes
  • Manage data breach notification obligations and coordinate required regulatory notifications
09
Identity Governance (IGA)
Intermediate

Identity Governance and Administration specialists manage who has access to what and ensure all access is appropriate, current, and compliant. They run access certification campaigns, enforce Segregation of Duties controls, implement role-based access models, and manage the full identity lifecycle.

SailPointSaviyntAccess ReviewsSoD ControlsRBAC
What You Do Daily
  • Run quarterly access certification campaigns where managers review and certify all user access
  • Implement SoD rules to prevent individuals from holding conflicting high-risk access combinations
  • Manage joiner/mover/leaver processes to ensure timely access provisioning and deprovisioning
  • Produce access compliance reports for auditors demonstrating adherence to least-privilege principles
10
Legal & Regulatory Requirements
Advanced

Legal and Regulatory specialists sit at the intersection of law and cybersecurity. They monitor global legislation changes, assess organizational impact, advise on contractual security obligations, manage regulatory relationships, and coordinate breach notifications and legal proceedings.

NIS2 DirectiveDORASEC Cyber RulesLegal CounselRegulatory Affairs
What You Do Daily
  • Monitor emerging cybersecurity laws (NIS2, DORA, SEC rules) and assess compliance obligations
  • Advise legal, procurement, and CISO on cybersecurity requirements in vendor and partner contracts
  • Coordinate mandatory breach notifications to regulators within legal deadlines (72-hour GDPR rule)
  • Support legal proceedings with forensic evidence requirements and chain-of-custody documentation
11
Security Awareness & Training
Beginner

Security Awareness specialists build programs that transform every employee into a security asset. They design training content, run phishing simulations, measure program effectiveness with metrics, and build a security culture that reduces human-layer risk — consistently the most exploited vulnerability.

KnowBe4Proofpoint SATPhishing SimulationseLearningCulture Change
What You Do Daily
  • Design and deliver engaging security awareness training covering phishing, password, and data handling
  • Run monthly phishing simulation campaigns and track click rates, reporting rates, and improvement trends
  • Create role-based training modules for high-risk groups: finance, IT admins, and executives
  • Report training completion rates, phishing metrics, and culture indicators to security leadership
12
Policy & Procedure Management
Beginner

Policy and Procedure Management specialists own the full lifecycle of security documentation — from drafting through review, approval, publication, and periodic refresh. They ensure all policies are clear, actionable, current, and reviewed regularly to stay aligned with evolving threats and regulations.

Document ControlGRC PlatformsPolicy LifecycleSharePointVersion Control

■ Career Tips & Certifications
Entry Cert
CompTIA Security+ + CISM (ISACA) is the standard GRC entry path. ISO 27001 Lead Implementer is highly valued.
Business Skills
GRC requires communication with executives. Strong writing and presentation skills are as important as technical knowledge.
Key Framework
Learn NIST CSF inside out first, then expand to ISO 27001 and relevant industry frameworks (PCI DSS, HIPAA, etc.).
Career Ceiling
GRC is one of the clearest paths to CISO. Risk management skills are essential at C-suite level.
